Clustering for Data Grouping

Problem: In many scenarios, datasets do not have labels. The goal is to automatically group similar data points based on their characteristics. Clustering helps identify hidden patterns and structures in the data.

Type: This belongs to Unsupervised Learning. One of the most common algorithms is K-Means.

Mathematical Model:

The objective of K-Means is to minimize the distance between data points and their assigned cluster centroid.

Process:

  • Select number of clusters (K)
  • Initialize centroids randomly
  • Assign each point to nearest centroid
  • Update centroids using mean of points
  • Repeat until convergence

Input:

  • Numerical dataset (CSV, database)
  • No labels required

Output:

  • Clusters
  • Cluster labels
  • Centroids

Evaluation:

  • Elbow Method (optimal K)
  • Silhouette Score

Relevance:

  • Customer segmentation
  • Medical data analysis
  • Agriculture (grouping livestock)
  • Anomaly detection

Advantages:

  • No labeled data needed
  • Simple and fast

Disadvantages:

  • Need to choose K
  • Sensitive to outliers

In conclusion, clustering is a fundamental technique in machine learning that combines statistical analysis and geometry to discover patterns in data.
